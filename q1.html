Emphasis and strengthened text

Some words in a sentence need more attention than others. If I tell you the quiz is on Thursday, I am stressing the day, not the quiz itself. If I say late submissions will not be accepted, that is information you cannot afford to miss. Emphasis changes how a sentence sounds, while strong text tells the reader that something is serious, urgent, or important.

... Separating content from presentation

Explanation

The <em> and <strong> tags are a better choice than <i> and <b> because they describe what the text means, not just how it should look. <em> signals stressed words, the way you would change your tone when speaking, and <strong> flags content that is important or urgent. <i> and <b> only tell the browser to show text in italics or bold, which is a decision about presentation. Keeping meaning in the HTML and leaving the look to CSS lets the page still make sense when it is read by a screen reader, scanned by a search engine, or restyled later. For example, if I decide important text should be red instead of bold, I can change one rule in my stylesheet and every <strong> element updates, while the markup still says exactly what that text is for.
