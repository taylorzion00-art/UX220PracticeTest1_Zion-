Emphasis and strengthened text

Okay, I know this is a hot take for some people, but Naruto is better than One Piece and I'm ready to defend it. The biggest reason is that Naruto actually ends. It wrapped up at chapter 700 back in 2014, so you get a full story with a real payoff. The One Piece manga has been going since 1997 and the anime is past a thousand episodes, which is a huge commitment before you even know how it finishes. As a business student I'm always thinking about opportunity cost, and my hours are a limited resource. Naruto also just hits harder. Watching a kid that the whole village ignored grow up to become Hokage is the underdog story, and the Pain arc still lives in my head rent free. I'll give One Piece credit for its worldbuilding, but if a friend asks me which one to start, it's Naruto every time.

... Separating content from presentation

Explanation

So why use <em> and <strong> instead of <i> and <b>? Basically, <em> and <strong> tell the browser what the text means, while <i> and <b> only change how it looks. When I put "ends" in an em tag, I'm stressing that word the same way I would if I was saying it out loud. When I used strong on "better than One Piece," I'm flagging that as my main point. Italics and bold on their own would look the same on screen, but there's no meaning behind them. That matters because not everyone experiences a page visually. Screen readers and search engines go off the code, not the styling. It also fits the whole idea of separating content from presentation, where HTML handles what the content is and CSS handles how it looks. If I ever wanted my strong text to be orange instead of bold (very on brand for Naruto), I'd just change the CSS and leave the HTML alone.
